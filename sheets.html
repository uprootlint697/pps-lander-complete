Why Big Brand Detergents Fail in Pet Households

Big detergent brands, Tide included, were made for human messes. Dirt, food stains, sweat.

These detergents used to be good enough, back when pets lived outside.

But nowadays, they live with us, and their fur is on our beds, our couches, and all over our clothes.

And pet hair is fundamentally different from anything else that gets on your clothes.

Here's why: pet hair is made of keratin, which is naturally water-resistant. The water and soap inside your machine slide right off it instead of washing it away.

As if that weren't enough, the outside of the hair is coated in a sticky substance called sebum. Sebum glues pet hair to fabric, along with saliva, dander, and other pet grime.

So the hair stays welded to your clothes. Or it breaks loose mid-cycle, drifts through the wash, and sticks to something else in the load.

Why does it stick right back on? Because nothing in your detergent ever dissolved the sebum. The hair that shakes loose is still coated in that same sticky film, so it glues itself to the next fabric it touches.

Either way, the fur that went in on your laundry comes back out on your "clean" clothes.

To truly solve the problem, you need to lift the sticky mess holding it in place and release the strands trapped in the fabric… So your washer can actually flush them away instead of moving them around.

Plain detergent, the stuff you've been using for years, was never designed to do any of this.
