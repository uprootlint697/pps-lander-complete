How Thousands Of Dog Parents Are Keeping Their Baby Safe By Petting Them

Posted By Mary Lee Aug 6, 2025

You wipe your feet when you come inside, but does your dog?

If you’ve noticed your dog licking their paws, scratching, or smelling strange, the real problem might be what’s stuck between their toes.

If you care about your pet’s comfort (and your family’s health) this could be the most eye-opening thing you read all week.

How often do you really clean your dog’s paws?

Most pet owners brush their dogs, or even give them baths every few weeks. But hardly anyone thinks about what’s hiding on their paws.

During spring and summer, your dog walks on hot pavement, lawn chemicals, fertilizer dust, and sticky pollen practically every day. That means their paws are picking up all kinds of nastiness. Even a short walk can leave grime, bacteria, and allergens trapped on their feet.

You often can’t even see the problem until it turns into itchy skin, constant licking, or even an infection. And while brushing helps the coat, and bathing sounds like a solution, it doesn’t do much for the problem area: their paws.

5 Ways Your Dog’s Paws Are Under Attack This Season

This season, your dog’s paws are in more danger than you think. Hot sidewalks cause painful burns. Lawn treatments and garden sprays soak into their pads. Pollen and mold cling to their fur. Dirty puddles can trigger infections.

And every time they lick their paws, they’re ingesting all of these toxins.
So what can you do to help them?

1Lawn Chemicals Are Hiding In Your Pup’s Paws

Most people don’t realize that pesticides and weed killers stay on grass for a long time.

When your dog walks across treated lawns, their paws soak up the residue. And since dogs groom by licking, that chemical buildup doesn’t stay on their skin:
it goes straight into their mouth.

Over time, this repeated exposure can affect digestion, cause irritation, or even impact liver health. Vets warn that consistent contact with lawn treatments could have long-term consequences, especially for smaller dogs.

Brushing won’t remove these chemicals. And even baths often won’t fully clean between the toes. If your dog spends any time outside, it’s worth thinking twice about what they’re really walking through.

2Allergens Make Your Dog’s Skin Itch Like Crazy

With spring comes airborne allergens like pollen, mold spores, and dust. These irritants stick to your dog’s paws, legs, and belly every time they go outside.

Pollen

Mold Spores

Dust

If your dog starts licking constantly, scratching at their skin, or develops red, irritated spots, seasonal allergens could be the cause. And if not taken care of, it can open the door to more serious problems, especially infections that thrive in damp, inflamed areas.

Dogs with sensitive skin or allergies are especially at risk. What starts as a harmless itch can quickly turn into raw, painful skin. Regular brushing might help a little, but it won’t reach the allergens trapped deep between the toes or under the belly. That’s why consistent, targeted cleaning is more important than ever this time of year.

3Harmful Bacteria Loves Muddy Paws

Your pup’s muddy paws aren’t just messy. In fact, they’re loaded with bacteria.

Dirty puddles and soggy grass are full of microbes that can cling to your dog’s feet and fur.

Once your pet steps into your home or car, that bacteria comes right along with them. And some of those germs can impact human health as well as dog health.

Frequent baths aren’t always the answer, though. Washing too often can dry out your dog’s skin and strip their coat of natural oils. That’s why it’s so important to gently clean their paws and belly after each outing - especially when things get muddy. A quick wipe-down can go a long way in keeping your home cleaner and your pet healthier.

4The Hidden Dangers of Pollen, Dust, And Mold

Pollen, dust, and spores aren’t just nuisances. These environmental allergens build up on the skin and fur, creating a constant source of irritation.

In dogs with sensitivities, this can lead to more than just itching or sneezing. It can cause respiratory issues, urinary discomfort, and irritation.

Dogs who spend a lot of time outdoors are especially vulnerable, making routine cleaning more important than ever.

5Invisible Irritants Can
Hitch A Ride On Even
“Clean” Dogs

Looking clean and actually being clean aren’t the same thing. Irritants can hide deep between the toes, in the undercoat, and around sensitive areas like the belly and tail… all places most brushes and wipes miss.

Most grooming routines only get dirt on the surface of your dog’s coat, and leave behind the irritants that actually pose a risk to your pup. Over time, these leftover particles lead to irritation or even infections that can be tough to treat.

That’s why it’s important to go beyond the surface for your pup’s safety. Proper grooming isn’t about appearances. It’s about clearing away the things you can’t see before they become a bigger problem.
