And once they’re cracked, it can be tough for dogs’ paws to heal. That’s because paw balms, aloe vera, and Vitamin E only moisturize the paws. They do nothing to actually heal the cracks once they’re formed.


To make matters worse, dogs don’t sit still to let you apply medicine to their paws, or stay off their feet long enough so the skin can heal.


It’s nearly impossible.


You may be wondering, what’s the harm in cracked paws? Cracked, brittle skin lets in all kinds of bacteria, germs, and infections.

In fact, even if you have an indoor dog, bacteria can still get into their paws! All it takes is a little bit of residue from outdoor shoes to infect your precious dog’s paws…

These researchers found that 72% of shoe soles and 42% of dog paws had enterobacteriaceae, an infection-causing bacteria. And that was after one 15-30 minute walk!1

So even if your dog stays inside all the time, you’re almost certainly leaving a trail of bacteria all over your floors.

Why Cracked Paw Pads Can Cause an Emergency Trip to the Vet

Skin is a protective organ: it holds in moisture and body fluids, as well as keeps out harmful bacteria.


Unfortunately, every crack in a dog's paw pad is an opportunity for infection to invade, bacteria and dirt to enter, causing painful infections, redness, and swelling. And it only gets worse if untreated.


Even if your dog stays inside, and even if you check your dog’s paws regularly, by the time a crack forms it’s already too late.
