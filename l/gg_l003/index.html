Are You Sabotaging Your Dog Every Time You Bathe Them?

Professional Groomer Reveals Why the Habit Most Dog Owners Swear By May Be Quietly Hurting Them

By Sarah Foster
Certified Professional Dog Groomer

Do you know how to tell if your dog is healthy?

I don’t mean “not sick.”

I mean healthy. Really healthy.

Because I do.

As someone who’s been a professional dog groomer for over a decade, I see dozens of dogs come through my salon every single week.

Most pet parents look for the telltale signs they already know: a healthy weight, eyes that are clear and free of discharge, and normal eating or drinking habits.

But there’s one big sign that any expert will tell you is a dead giveaway…

And it’s probably not something you think about very often.

It’s their coat.

And, if you’re like most of my clients, you’re probably taking care of your dog’s coat all wrong.

It’s not your fault. You didn’t fail your dog.

It’s just that you were never taught that there was a better way.

Why Their Coat Is the Key to Their Health

Here's why vets pay so much attention to it.

Their coat isn't just fur. It's their body's first line of defense.

  • It protects their skin from sun, cold, and irritation.
  • It regulates their temperature, keeping them warm in winter and cooler in summer.
  • It's a physical barrier - a wall against bacteria, parasites, and allergens that would otherwise have direct access to their skin.

When your dog’s coat is healthy, everything underneath is more likely to be healthy, too.

But when their coat gets dry, brittle, and damaged…

That wall breaks down.

Leaving their skin vulnerable.

Worst of all, though, a dog with a dull coat and dry skin is a miserable dog. Constantly itching and scratching. Unable to get comfortable.

But if you’re like many well-meaning pet parents…

Every time you noticed their coat looking dull or dry, or you saw them licking or scratching, what did you do?

You gave your dog a bath.

You just didn’t realize that the bath was causing the exact problems it was intended to solve.

But You're Supposed to Bathe Your Dog…Right?

Bathing your dog isn't a bad thing, and you’re not a bad pet parent if you do it.

But here’s the thing: most of the time you’re bathing the dog for your sake, not theirs.

Think about it…

Biologists believe we domesticated dogs about 20,000 years ago. We only started bathing them in the last few decades.

Before that, they were perfectly capable of taking care of their coats on their own.

How did they do it?

They replenished the natural oils in their skin and coat by rolling around in the grass or rubbing on bark and brush. Plants have natural oils that are extremely beneficial for dogs and their coats.

Dog shampoos, on the other hand…

Let’s just say dog shampoos are certainly not “natural.”

So why did we invent them?

Because our dogs started doing other things they didn’t evolve to do. Like living in our homes, sleeping in our beds, and lying on our expensive couches…

Suddenly, a stinky, filthy dog wasn’t a problem that you could just ignore.

People just wanted the smell and grime gone…

And so we invented special dog shampoos with harsh chemicals that were fantastic at stripping away dirt and odor.

Unfortunately, as we’ve since learned…

Those shampoos also strip away a lot of things your dog needs to be healthy.

What Dog Shampoo Really Does to Your Dog

Before I can show you what dog shampoo does to your dog, I first need to explain what dog shampoo actually is.

Every dog shampoo on the shelf - moisturizing, hypoallergenic, even the ones labeled “natural” or “gentle” - works the same basic way.

  • They use sulfates to create lather.
  • Most have parabens to keep the formula stable on the shelf.
  • And many include a dash of synthetic fragrance that makes him smell clean for a day or two.

These ingredients strip away the natural oils that form a protective barrier for his skin.

That’s not a flaw. It’s not one bad brand.

It’s how shampoo is designed to work.

Remember, when these shampoos were invented, people didn’t know about natural oils or protecting skin barriers.

They just wanted their dogs to be clean. No matter what.

So dog shampoos were designed to remove everything from your dog’s fur — even the good stuff.

If your shampoo creates lather, then it’s doing more than just removing dirt. It’s also breaking down your dog’s natural oils — there's simply no version of “gentle cleansing” that gets around that completely.

And most people don’t feel like a shampoo is working unless there’s A LOT of lather.

As for the other ingredients, you’ve probably noticed your own skincare products claiming to be free of parabens and artificial fragrances…

There’s a good reason for that.

We’re not saying you should never bathe your dog. Sometimes there’s no getting around it.

But know that, when you do, you’re essentially dropping a bomb on their natural defenses.

That can lead to irritation, allergies, and infections…

And the more irritated the skin gets, the more your dog licks and scratches…

Which causes many owners to reach for the shampoo that caused all these problems in the first place.

I know, it sounds crazy to hear from a professional groomer, but…dog shampoo should be used sparingly, if at all.

Especially since there’s a better way.

I found that out by necessity.

I kept seeing the same dogs come back with dull coats, irritated skin, and other issues — even though I’d just groomed them a few weeks earlier.

At first, I did what I always did — which was to use more shampoo. Lather them up even more.

Eventually, though, I began to suspect the shampoo might be the problem.

So I switched to a “gentle” version. Then a medicated shampoo. Even an all-natural, oatmeal-based formula specifically for dogs with irritated skin.

None of it worked.

Their coats were still dull. Their skin was still irritated. If anything, it was worse.

One night, frustrated after yet another one of my regular dogs showed up with a ragged coat and itchy skin, I did some digging.

This time, I wasn’t looking into new shampoo brands.

I was investigating shampoo itself.

That’s when I learned how it works, and what it really does.

And that’s when I stopped reaching for the shampoo bottle.
